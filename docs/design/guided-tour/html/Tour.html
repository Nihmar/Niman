The guided tour

Issue #266 asks for a guided tour of the app's features: one step at a time, each step pointing at the real control. This file draws the desktop shape, the offer, where the tour comes back, and the playground. The phone has its own file, Phone.html.

The welcome deck introduces the app; the tour is act two — the same ground again, over a real library. Everything dims; the control being talked about does not. A step's target is measured from the widget itself, so the hole lands on the real control, not on a picture of one.

One scrim, one hole, one card. The dim is the app's scrim at 55%; the hole is the control's own rectangle inflated 6 px, edged in 2 px of the accent at a 12 px radius. The card carries the step counter (n of m), the heading, the prose, and Skip — then Back and Next at its foot, the last step's button reading Done. A phone walk is 7 steps, a wide window 8: the dock is the one a phone has not got.

4 — the offer · the deck asks, the shell confirms

The tour is offered once, and only when a library is open to point at. The deck's last page carries the choice; the shell shows the dialog after the first library opens. Both are skippable, and the offer is spent when shown — declining leaves the tour to Help and the palette, and nothing is asked twice.

6 — the playground · the cheatsheet, not a sample note

The tour writes nothing of its own. Its last step hands over to the cheatsheet (#265): the walk ends, the cheatsheet opens, and each example can be put in the note already open. No sample note is created in anyone's library — the user keeps only what they choose to Insert.

The cheatsheet, written beside shown, with Copy and Insert in the note on every row — the icon on the right. It is a page the app already has; the tour only opens it and ends, so there is nothing of the tour's own to clean up afterwards. Why the cheatsheet and not a sample note: a note the tour made would either be left behind in the library or have to be deleted afterwards; the cheatsheet is already there, it teaches the syntax rather than one note's words, and the user leaves with exactly what they chose to insert.

Mockups, not screenshots of the app: everything here is HTML drawn to look like Niman, using the real palette from lib/src/ui/theme/niman.dart (night). Where a mockup and the app disagree, the app ships — these record what was drawn, and what it asks. The tour's steps, targets and overlay live in lib/src/ui/tour/; the offer and the entries in lib/src/ui/tour/tour_host.dart.
